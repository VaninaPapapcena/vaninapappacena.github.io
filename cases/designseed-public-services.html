Social Impact & Mentorship: The "Design Seeds" Programs

Technical students had the hard skills, but not the frameworks to see the social impact of what they built. This work closed that gap through a curriculum built around purpose and systemic thinking.

Project summary

Connect technical skills with social purpose

Technical students from the Buenos Aires City Government’s professional internship program possessed strong hard skills but lacked the frameworks to understand the social impact of their creations. The goal was to instill a systemic mindset in these future professionals through specialized workshops — teaching them to map the consequences of their technology on community and environment.

  • FocusSocial impact workshops for the City Government's professional internship program (Prácticas Profesionalizantes)
  • MethodTwo hands-on workshops on purpose-driven project development
  • Result80% of students engaged to continue applying the model

Role

Design and facilitate impact workshops, instill a systemic mindset/h2>
  • Designed and facilitated a two-part workshop series and impact measurement framework integrated into public technical schools.
  • Set the goal of teaching students to map the consequences of their technology on community and environment.
  • Used clearer layouts and flows to reduce ambiguity in decision-making.

Approach

Two workshops, one shift in mindset

Workshop 1 — Theory of Change

  • Students learned to plan their internship projects starting from a deep sense of purpose, ensuring each technical step had meaning and social direction.

Workshop 2 — Impact Models

  • Introduced tools to help students consider the social and environmental impact of their technical solutions from the very beginning.

Outcome

A mindset shift, backed by real engagement

High engagement

Achieved 80% student interest in continuing the "Theory of Change" model within their projects.

Actionable toolkits

Delivered scalable impact frameworks that technical schools under the Buenos Aires City Government can adopt autonomously.

Talent pipeline

Mentored the next generation of technical professionals to think ethically and systemically before entering the workforce.

Who this is for

  • Educational institutions & technical schools
  • Public sector & government internship programs
  • Incubation and acceleration programs
  • Companies seeking to develop tech talent with a systemic, impact-oriented mindset

Key learning

There's a clear gap between technical knowledge and the ability to create real-world impact. Introducing these frameworks early in high school professional internships helps students build more conscious, sustainable, and context-aware solutions.
