Civic Tech & Blockchain:Translating emerging technologies into tangible value propositions for citizen participation.

Project summary

Demystifying Blockchain to unlock real public value

Blockchain technology was highly promising but deeply abstract for government applications. The challenge was moving past the market "hype" to design an experience that solved concrete citizen needs and built real, verifiable public trust.

  • FocusBridging the gap between complex, emerging technology and user-centered civic solutions.
  • MethodCivic co-creation workshops, public sector service mapping, and participatory prototyping
  • ResultA validated digital proof-of-concept for secure, decentralized citizen data management.

Role

Translating complex technology into human-centered design

  • I led the translation of emerging tech into clear user needs, ensuring we weren't just building technology for technology’s sake, but answering real civic problems.
  • I designed and facilitated co-creation workshops, bringing together non-technical neighbors and public officials to identify where decentralized trust was actually needed.
  • I advocated for ethical design principles, defining strict boundaries for data privacy, user autonomy, and transparency from the very first sketch.

Approach

Bridging the gap between neighbors and complex architecture

We designed an immersion and co-creation process hosted directly inside local community centers and training spaces, structured around three core moments:

  • Accessible Demystification: We stripped away the technical jargon, explaining what Blockchain is through tangible, real-world analogies that non-technical users could fully grasp.
  • Neighbor Integration Spaces: We created safe collaborative environments where diverse citizens could openly discuss their friction points and systemic trust issues with public services.
  • Co-Designed Prototyping: We facilitated interactive design activities where users directly co-created ideas and validated features centered on public transparency.

Outcome

From abstract concept to a citizen-validated framework

Validated Proof of Concept

Developed the "Citizen Folder" (Carpeta Ciudadana) concept, fully grounded in verified user needs rather than technical assumptions.

Tech Adoption Strategy

Created a clear framework to communicate decentralized benefits and privacy mechanics to everyday, non-technical citizens.

Responsible Design

Defined the boundaries for data privacy and transparency in the prototype.

Who this is for

  • Local governments and public sectors looking to implement emerging tech responsibly.
  • Civic tech initiatives and public innovation labs seeking to drive participatory design.
  • Product and design teams needing frameworks to co-create complex tech with real end-users

Key learning

Involving end-users from day one drastically improves the relevance of complex technical products; if the citizen doesn't understand the value of decentralized trust, the technology fails. Furthermore, these participatory civic spaces require immense strategic planning, as they are notoriously difficult to sustain within the tight timeline constraints of a government term.
